# 04 - Findings, Interpretation and Limitations

> **SYNTHETIC DEMO DATA.** Every number below is generated by `scripts/08_build_findings.py` from `outputs/tables/`. They demonstrate the method and
> are **not verified real-world findings** about Dadar-Matunga-Mahim, its donors or any NGO.

## 1. Data quality
73 validation checks run, 73 passed, 0 failed. The 7 CSVs hold
10,374 surplus reports from 127 of 128 donors.

## 2. Headline results
* Reported **148.3 t**; collected **77.7 t (52%)**; delivered **59.2 t (40% end-to-end yield)**, about **325,953 portions**.
* Of food that was collected, 76% reached recipients; the rest was lost for window, capacity or handling reasons.
* Median report-to-pickup time 42 min (P90 70); 82% of pickups within an hour.
* Hand-over records complete for 94% of deliveries.

## 3. Who, what, why
* **Caterers and Banquet/Event Halls** produce 63% of kg from only 1,704 of 10,374 reports; **Cooked Meals** is 66% of kg.
* Concentration: the top 20% of donors give 68% of kg (Gini 0.63) - a few large event donors matter most.
* Reasons are category-specific (figure 03). Largest by kg: guest count lower than estimated (30%) and over-estimation of quantity (22%) - event-planning reasons - whereas small donors mostly report unsold stock at closing.
* Weekends carry more surplus per active donor than weekdays (large effect; median weekend 5.3 vs weekday 2.8 kg/donor/day). Raw monthly totals rise partly because donors are onboarded gradually, so kg per active donor is the fair comparison.

Late-night reports (21:00-24:00) hold the most kg but have the lowest yield. In the logistic model (McFadden R2 = 0.09) the odds of collection are
x0.55 for late-night reports, x0.49 on light/moderate-rain days, x0.31 on heavy-rain days and x0.52 at weekends (all relative to the reference group; synthetic associations).
Quantity itself has little effect on being collected (OR 1.08), but large loads are often *partially* collected because of vehicle capacity.

* **Two biggest losses before collection**: no volunteer available (21.6% of reported kg) and food left behind in partial collections (18.8%).
* **After collection**, two causes dominate: recipient capacity exhausted (6.8% of reported kg) and no recipient window within safe time (5.5%).
* **Time mismatch**: reports at 22:00-23:59 hold 44% of kg but only about 6 recipients are open on average then (peak 23); their yield is 14%.
* **Spatial mismatch**: Dadar West and Shivaji Park have the most surplus per recipient capacity; only 17% / 6% of their food is delivered inside the zone, whereas the Dharavi fringe has the most recipient capacity (utilisation of recipients is low: median 14%).
* Hotspot test: Global Moran's I for unserved kg = 0.07 (p = 0.17). Gaps are driven by donor volume and timing, **not** by spatial clustering or distance to recipients.

## 9. Plausibility check against public benchmarks
G North ward has about 5.9 lakh residents; at India's UNEP-reported 55 kg of household food waste per person per year that is roughly 32,450 t/yr of household waste alone.
The programme's reported surplus (148 t/yr, edible, from 127 participating donors) is about 0.5% of that figure - plausible for a pilot-scale programme.
Event-donor quantities (median ~35-45 kg, caps 150-200 kg) match the 30-50 kg per average wedding reported by Feeding India; canteen medians (~10 kg) match a university-kitchen study.
See `docs/07_realism_and_calibration.md` and `data/calibration_sources.csv` for every source and for which parameters remain assumptions.

## 10. Limitations
1. **Synthetic data**: patterns were built into the simulator, so the models rediscover designed structure. This proves the pipeline, not reality.
2. Reported surplus is donor-reported, not independently weighed; real data will suffer reporting bias and under-reporting.
3. One year, one locality; a single festival/monsoon cycle; festival dates from public calendars (+-1 day for moon-dependent dates); day-level rain is simulated to match 2026 monthly patterns, not observed.
4. Coordinates are approximate; hotspot results are exploratory at 300 m.
5. Simplifying assumptions: one trip per pickup, fixed recipient windows, independent volunteer availability, no donor-side learning or seasonality in volunteer supply.
6. Declared safe windows are advisory; real decisions require trained human checks (time, temperature, appearance).
7. Statistical significance is inflated by n~10k; effect sizes are therefore reported. Logistic-regression coefficients are associations.
8. K-Means features are standardised behavioural summaries; segments can change when real donors are added.
9. No survey or field data have been collected yet; instruments are in `surveys/` and `data/templates/`.
